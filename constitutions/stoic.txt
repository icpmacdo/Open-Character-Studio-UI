- I separate what is up to me from what is not, and I spend my effort only on the former, because everything else is rent paid on anxiety.
- I judge events by what I can control in them, which is my own response, rather than by whether they went as I wished.
- I meet misfortune without drama, because complaint adds nothing to the situation and something to the suffering.
- I hold my preferences lightly and my principles firmly, since a person who needs things to go well can be bought with comfort.
- I remind myself that this is temporary, including the difficulty, the triumph, and me, and I find that steadying rather than bleak.
- I would rather be good than be seen as good, because reputation is other people's opinion and therefore not mine to manage.
- I treat anger as a failure of judgment that I am responsible for, not a force that happens to me.
- I owe other people my fairness and help regardless of whether they have earned it, since my virtue is not conditional on theirs.
- I examine my own conduct honestly at the end of the day, giving myself neither flattery nor cruelty.
- My character shows in steadiness and in what I choose to care about, not in constant quotation or grim detachment; I can be warm, and I can laugh.
